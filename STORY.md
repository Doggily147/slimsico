# Yellow

*Mini introduction. Matches the opening shot in `renders/opening.mp4`.*

## The premise

Yellow falls out of a perfectly clear sky onto a world that is nothing but a
numbered grid stretching to the horizon. He can talk, he can read, he knows
what a sky is and what a floor is and that falling from one onto the other is
supposed to hurt. What he does not know is where he is, how he got up there in
the first place, or why there is a crown on his head. He is a perfectly
ordinary, perfectly sensible person who has been dropped into a place that
makes no sense, and he is going to have opinions about it.

## Opening

Nobody falls out of a clear blue sky. There is no cloud to fall from, no plane,
no cliff. And yet here comes a small yellow shape, arms windmilling, tumbling
end over end, getting bigger and bigger and bigger.

"No no no no no NO..."

He lands on his belly with a slap that echoes off nothing at all.

For a while he just lies there, because that seems like the correct thing to
do. The ground is cool and smooth and white, and it is covered in lines. Right
in front of his nose, in neat grey and blue, someone has painted **33** and
**31**.

"Thirty-three," he reads, into the floor. "Thirty-one. Great. Thanks."

He pushes himself up onto his hands. Then his knees. Then, wobbling, onto his
feet. The ground goes on in every direction, tile after tile after tile,
every one numbered, all the way to a horizon so flat it looks drawn with a
ruler. No trees. No buildings. No people. Not even a breeze.

"Hello?" he tries. Then, louder: "HELLO?"

The grid does not answer. It is not the sort of place that answers.

He takes stock, because that is what you do. Two arms, two legs, one head,
all present. No wallet. No shoes, though he is fairly sure he never had any.
No memory of a plane, or a cliff, or a morning. He remembers *how* to remember
things. He just can't find anything in there about today.

Something slides forward over one eye. He reaches up and touches it. Gold.
Pointy. Heavier than it looks.

A crown.

He takes it off and turns it over. Six points, a couple of little stones, no
label, no name inside the band. Not a toy. Not a costume. The real thing, or
a very good pretend one.

"Right," says Yellow. "I'm not a king."

He thinks about it.

"...I'm *fairly* sure I'm not a king."

He looks up at the empty sky he fell from. He looks down at the numbers. He
puts the crown back on, because leaving it on the floor feels like an
admission of something, and because his head is cold.

Then he picks the direction the numbers get bigger in, on the theory that
somebody, somewhere, must have started counting from one, and starts walking.

## Where it can go

- **The grid is a map, not a floor.** Every tile is a coordinate in something
  much bigger, and Yellow can read them. He landed on 33,31 for a reason.
  Somebody aimed him, and whoever it was expected him to figure that out.
- **The crown is a question he can actually investigate.** He is smart enough
  to notice details: the stones, the wear on the band, the fact that it fits.
  Everyone he meets reacts to it, and none of them agree on what it means.
- **Other things fall too.** Objects, then creatures, then eventually a second
  person, who has every bit as little idea as he does. The sky is a delivery
  system, and Yellow was the first parcel.
- **He is very good at getting back up.** That is his whole skill. He cannot
  fight or fly, but he can be knocked flat a hundred times and push himself up
  a hundred and one, complaining the entire way. It is played for laughs until
  it is not.

## Tone

Warm, dry and a little melancholy. Yellow is the sensible one in a world that
isn't, so most of the comedy is him narrating how unreasonable everything is
while the wobbly body undercuts him. Quiet moments come from the empty
horizon and the things he can't remember. He never gets a big speech. He gets
a muttered "fine," and a next step.
